Section 01 // Core Concept

Three key skills

You'll need to move between expanded and factorised forms of expressions. Think of it like zooming in and out on the same idea.

Expanding single brackets

Multiply everything inside by the term outside.
3(x + 4) = 3x + 12. Don't miss the second term!

Expanding double brackets

Use FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last — or the area grid.
(x+3)(x+2) = x² + 5x + 6.

Factorising

Find the HCF of all terms and take it outside.
6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3). Check by expanding back.

Like terms

Collect terms with the same variable and power.
3x² + 5x + 2x² − x = 5x² + 4x. Only like with like.

Section 03 // Factorising Tricks

Spotting the common factor

🔍

Find the HCF

6x² + 9x — HCF of 6 and 9 is 3; both have an x → 3x(2x + 3). Always pull out the highest common factor.

🔁

Check by expanding

After factorising, expand again. If you get the original expression back, you got it right. Always verify.

⚡

Difference of two squares

x² − 9 = x² − 3² = (x+3)(x−3). Spot it: two perfect squares subtracted.

📊

Substitution check

Sub a number into both sides. x = 2: x² − 9 = −5; (2+3)(2−3) = 5 × (−1) = −5. ✓

Section 04 // Encrypted: GCSE Stretch

Advanced data

🔒 Factorising quadratics & index laws
Factorising x² + bx + c: find two numbers that add to b and multiply to c. x² + 5x + 6 = (x+2)(x+3) because 2+3=5, 2×3=6.
Index laws: aˣ × aʸ = aˣ⁺ʸ · aˣ ÷ aʸ = aˣ⁻ʸ · (aˣ)ʸ = aˣʸ. Apply to simplify x³ × x⁵ = x⁸.
Negative and fractional indices: a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ; a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a. So 27^(1/3) = ∛27 = 3 and 4⁻² = 1/16.

Simplifying algebraic fractions: factorise numerator and denominator, then cancel common factors. (x²−4)/(x+2) = (x+2)(x−2)/(x+2) = x−2.
