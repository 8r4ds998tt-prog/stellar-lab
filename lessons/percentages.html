Section 01 // Core Concept

Three problems, one idea

Every percentage problem is about comparing a part to a whole. There are three types you need:

Find X% of Y

Convert the percentage to a decimal: ÷ 100, then multiply.
e.g. 30% of 80 = 0.3 × 80 = 24

Percentage increase

Multiply by a multiplier greater than 1.
e.g. Increase £60 by 20%: 60 × 1.20 = £72

Percentage decrease

Multiply by a multiplier less than 1.
e.g. Decrease £60 by 20%: 60 × 0.80 = £48

Percentage change

Change ÷ original × 100.
e.g. From 50 to 65: 15 ÷ 50 × 100 = 30% increase

Section 03 // Real Life

Where percentages hide

🏷️

Shop discounts

A £80 jacket is 25% off → 80 × 0.75 = £60. The multiplier is always 1 − (% ÷ 100).

🏦

VAT (20%)

Add 20% VAT to a price: multiply by 1.20. Remove VAT: divide by 1.20 (reverse percentage).

📊

Exam marks

Scored 48 out of 60: % = 48 ÷ 60 × 100 = 80%. Change ÷ original × 100 always works.

Section 04 // Encrypted: GCSE Stretch

Advanced data

🔒 Compound interest & reverse percentages
Compound interest: apply the multiplier repeatedly. £500 at 3% for 4 years: 500 × 1.03⁴ = £562.75. Each year's interest earns interest too.
Reverse percentage: you know the final amount after a % change — find the original. After a 20% increase, divide by 1.20. After a 15% decrease, divide by 0.85.
Multiplier method: any chain of percentage changes can be combined by multiplying all multipliers together. +20% then −10% = 1.20 × 0.90 = 1.08 overall, so an 8% net increase.

Depreciation uses repeated multiplication below 1. A car worth £12,000 losing 18% per year: after 3 years = 12000 × 0.82³ ≈ £6,602.
