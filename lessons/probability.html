Section 01 // Core Concept

The probability scale

Probability is written as a fraction, decimal, or percentage between 0 and 1 (or 0% and 100%). The closer to 1, the more likely the event.

0Impossible
0.25Unlikely
0.5Even chance
0.75Likely
1Certain
0Roll a 7 on a normal dice
⅙Roll a 1 on a fair dice
½Flip heads on a coin
⅚Not roll a 1 on a dice
1Roll ≤ 6 on a normal dice

Theoretical probability

Based on equally likely outcomes: P(event) = favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes. For a fair coin: P(H) = 1/2.

Experimental (relative frequency)

Based on results from actual trials: P ≈ frequency ÷ number of trials. More trials → closer to the theoretical value (Law of Large Numbers).

Complementary events

P(not A) = 1 − P(A). If the chance of rain is 0.3, the chance of no rain is 0.7. They always sum to 1.

Mutually exclusive events

Events that can't happen at the same time. P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). Rolling a 2 and rolling a 5 on one throw are mutually exclusive.

Section 04 // Encrypted: GCSE Stretch

Advanced data

🔒 Venn diagrams & independent / dependent events
Independent events: the outcome of one doesn't affect the other. P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Tossing a coin twice — each toss is independent.
Dependent events (conditional): sampling without replacement changes the probabilities. If bag has 3 red, 2 blue — P(red 1st) = 3/5; P(red 2nd | red 1st) = 2/4 because one red is gone.
Venn diagrams: circles represent sets/events. The intersection (∩) is "A AND B". The union (∪) is "A OR B". P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B).

Expected frequency: probability × number of trials. If P(6) = 1/6 and you roll 300 times, you'd expect 300 × 1/6 = 50 sixes.
