Section 01 // Core Concept

2D and 3D space

Area measures the surface inside a 2D shape (cm², m²). Volume measures how much 3D space a solid fills (cm³, m³). Get the formula right, check your units, and substitute.

Area formulae (2D)

Rectangle: l × w · Triangle: ½ × b × h · Circle: πr² · Trapezium: ½(a+b)h · Parallelogram: b × h

Volume formulae (3D)

Cuboid: l × w × h · Cylinder: πr²h · Cone: ⅓πr²h · Sphere: ⁴⁄₃πr³ · Prism: cross-section area × length

Surface area

The total area of all faces. For a cuboid: 2(lw + lh + wh). For a cylinder: 2πr² + 2πrh (two circles + curved surface).

Units matter

Length in cm → area in cm², volume in cm³. If you mix units, convert first. 1 m² = 10,000 cm². 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³.

Section 04 // Encrypted: GCSE Stretch

Advanced data

🔒 Sphere, cone & composite shapes
Sphere: Volume = ⁴⁄₃πr³; Surface area = 4πr². These won't be given in the exam — memorise them.
Cone: Volume = ⅓πr²h; Curved surface area = πrl where l is the slant height (use Pythagoras: l² = r² + h²).
Composite shapes: split into simpler parts, find each area/volume, add (or subtract). e.g. L-shape = large rectangle − small rectangle.

Reverse problems: rearrange the formula. Cylinder volume 200 cm³, radius 4 cm → h = 200 ÷ (π×4²) ≈ 3.98 cm.
