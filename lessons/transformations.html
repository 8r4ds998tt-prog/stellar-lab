Section 01 // Core Concept

Four transformations

A transformation changes the position or size of a shape. The original is called the object; the result is called the image. Three of the four are isometries — the shape stays the same size.

Translation

Slides the shape — every point moves the same distance in the same direction. Described by a vector (a, b): right/left = a, up/down = b.

Rotation

Turns the shape around a centre of rotation. Described by: centre, angle, and direction (clockwise or anticlockwise).

Reflection

Flips the shape over a mirror line. Each point is the same distance from the line on the other side. Common lines: x-axis, y-axis, y = x, y = −x.

Enlargement

Changes size by a scale factor from a centre of enlargement. SF > 1 → bigger; 0 < SF < 1 → smaller. Negative SF → opposite side.

Section 03 // Describing Fully

Say exactly what happened

➡️

Describe a translation

"Translation by vector (3, −2)" — three right, two down. You must give the vector, not just the direction.

🔄

Describe a rotation

"Rotation of 90° anticlockwise about the origin (0, 0)." Centre, angle and direction are all required.

🪞

Describe a reflection

"Reflection in the line y = x." Name the mirror line — always an equation.

🔍

Describe an enlargement

"Enlargement scale factor 3, centre (0, 0)." Both scale factor and centre are required.

Section 04 // Encrypted: GCSE Stretch

Advanced data

🔒 Negative scale factors & combined transformations
Negative scale factor: an enlargement with SF = −2 maps the image to the opposite side of the centre, at twice the distance. The image is inverted.
Fractional scale factor: SF = ½ makes the image half the size. Still called an enlargement even though it gets smaller.
Combined transformations: e.g. reflect then rotate. Order matters — reflect then rotate ≠ rotate then reflect in general. Describe each step fully.

Invariant points: points that don't move under a transformation. A point on the mirror line is invariant under reflection. The centre of rotation is invariant under rotation.
